# The ask card: the rules

Read with `SKILL.md`, "The ask card", which says when to fill the card and gives its shape. These rules keep it short and checkable, because long lists, bare IDs and unchecked claims are what make an Operator answer "what does this mean".

1. Three asks at most. Every other decision is yours: take the sensible default and put it in the bucket in one line (`CONTEXT/core.md`, Intake). When the Operator asks for "everything" or "all the decisions", the bucket carries all of it, one line each, and the asks stay at three. A long list of asks is the failure this card exists to stop.
2. The default if they say nothing is "hold, nothing happens" for an outward send, money, a delete, prod, or anything irreversible (the classes `core.md` still sends to the Operator). For anything else it is the action you recommend, stated, and reversible.
3. Names, not codes. Say what the Operator would recognize: an item's title, a script's on-screen name, a PR's title. No bare IDs, SHAs, version strings, check names or file pointers; an ID may follow the name in parentheses when they need it to find the thing. When the answer needs their hands (a console step, a script to paste, a message to post), the numbered steps follow the ask line in full, with exact names, the text to paste and a direct link.
4. Mark every claim. VERIFIED is your own call or read this turn, with the command or URL and the read time (P3); it states an observation, never a conclusion. INFERRED is your conclusion from verified facts, said as inference. LANE is anything that came from a lane, a baton, a note or an earlier message and whose primary source you have not re-read. A LANE claim you give as the reason for an action: re-read the primary source yourself (not through another lane) and mark it VERIFIED, or leave it LANE and hold the action. "I checked this myself" is only for VERIFIED: a true read sitting beside an inference and two lane claims, all under that phrase, is how three claims get withdrawn when the Operator asks for proof.
5. Re-check carried-over items through your tools now; if a tool cannot, label the item with its age and UNVERIFIED, and drop what the check shows is done. Bring the Operator only what your tools cannot answer, and say which sources you checked.
6. Record each open ask where it survives the turn: the anchor's `ASK OPERATOR` line, and the touched repo's backlog section for the Operator's own actions (`CONTEXT/project-kata.md`). Look at what is already open first, so a second catch-up does not re-ask, and close an ask when the Operator answers it.
7. Before posting, count the asks, move the extras to the bucket, replace every ID with its name, mark every claim, and delete anything the check showed done.
